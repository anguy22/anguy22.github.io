Ramblings of the Deranged

Markov Property

How do we think of our past?

Click for details

If memoryless distributions rely only on the current state and hold any past states fixed, no probability is accumulated at any previous point. Nothing in the past can change. Future states only rely on the exact moment in time we evaluate. This suggests that the past matters only insofar as it produced the present state. Once we arrive here, the next transition depends on where we are now, not the full path that brought us here. What, then, should we carry forward: memory, or state?

Bellman Principle

Are we on our best possible path?

Click for details

If a path is truly optimal, then every remaining decision after the first step must also be optimal for the new state created by that step. The best possible path is not judged by whether every prior step was perfect. It is judged by whether, from the state you are in now your remaining decisions form the best possible continuation. When we consider this iteratively, is no decision ever a bad decision, only a necessary one?

Benford's Law

Is random random?

Click for details

If a set of naturally occurring numbers spans multiple orders of magnitude, then smaller leading digits will appear more often than larger leading digits. This reflects order in seemingly random distributions, despite attempts at randomness and unbiased sampling. If something appears random, it may still obey a hidden distribution shaped by scale, growth, and constraint. This suggests chaos carries structure when viewed at the right level. What, then, is truly random?
