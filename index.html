Markets

—
The columns running across the floor outside are a procedural animation, not live quotes. No price is shown against them anywhere on this page.
  • Designs and manufactures DRAM and NAND flash memory. One of three remaining DRAM makers at scale, alongside Samsung and SK Hynix.
  • Memory is sold as a commodity, so pricing moves with the balance of bit supply against bit demand rather than with individual design wins.
  • Supplies HBM — high-bandwidth memory stacked beside AI accelerators — which is allocated under long-term agreements rather than sold spot.
  • Designs and manufactures x86 processors for PCs and servers, and sells discrete accelerators and networking silicon.
  • Intel Foundry manufactures for external customers as well as for Intel’s own product lines, reported as a separate operating segment.
  • One of the few firms that both designs and fabricates at the leading edge, which makes it capital-intensive in a way fabless designers are not.
  • Builds etch and deposition equipment used to cut and build the structures on a wafer, plus wafer-cleaning systems.
  • Etch demand scales with 3D NAND layer counts — each additional layer adds process steps to the same wafer.
  • Revenue tracks industry wafer fab equipment spending, which is set by memory and logic capex budgets a year or more ahead.
  • The largest semiconductor equipment maker by revenue, and the one with the broadest product range.
  • Sells deposition, etch, ion implantation, chemical-mechanical planarisation and inspection systems, plus display manufacturing equipment.
  • Serves both leading-edge and trailing-edge nodes, and runs a services business tied to the installed base rather than to new tool orders.
  • Supplies inspection and metrology systems that find defects and measure features between the steps of a fabrication flow.
  • Holds the majority share of the process-control segment, well ahead of its nearest competitors.
  • Inspection intensity rises as feature sizes shrink: smaller structures and more process steps mean more points at which a wafer has to be measured.
  • The sole supplier of extreme ultraviolet lithography systems. No leading-edge logic or memory is patterned without one.
  • High‑NA EUV is the next generation, raising numerical aperture to print smaller features in a single exposure.
  • Unit volumes are low and unit prices run into the hundreds of millions, so a handful of shipments moves a quarter. Sales are also subject to export controls.
  • Designs GPUs and data-centre accelerators. Fabless — it designs but does not manufacture, outsourcing fabrication to foundries.
  • Sells systems and networking alongside the silicon, and the CUDA software stack that most of the training ecosystem is written against.
  • Accelerator demand propagates upstream: an order here becomes HBM allocation, then wafer starts, then equipment bookings, with a lag at each step.
